Accounting hiring managers want to see three things quickly: your credentials, the size and type of work you handle, and whether you make the close faster and cleaner. This template uses the Two Column layout. Experience and education sit in the main column, while skills, certifications and languages sit in a narrow side column where they are easy to scan.

The sample belongs to a senior accountant with a CPA and a background in audit. Use it as a starting point and replace it with your own details.

## What hiring managers look for

A controller or finance manager reading your resume is usually asking a few plain questions. Can this person run their part of the close without supervision? Do they know our standards and systems? Will the auditors find problems in their work?

Your resume should answer those questions in the first few lines of each job. Say what you own, the size of the books and how you improved the process. Accounting is a field where precision is the job, so a clean, consistent resume with correct numbers is evidence in itself.

Recruiters screen first, and many of them search for credentials and system names. Write CPA, ACCA, CMA, US GAAP, IFRS, SAP or NetSuite exactly the way they appear in job posts.

## How to write each section

### Header and contact details

Your name, your credential, the role you want, city, email, phone and a LinkedIn link. "Priya Raman, CPA" or a title line like "Senior Accountant, CPA" puts the credential where nobody can miss it. You don't need a full address or a photo for jobs in the US, the UK or Canada.

### Summary

Two or three sentences with your credential, years of experience, the kind of work you own and one thing you are good at. These examples show the pattern at different levels:

> **Staff or entry level:** Staff accountant with two years in accounts payable and bank reconciliations for a mid-size distributor. CPA candidate, passed FAR and AUD, and comfortable with NetSuite and Excel.

> **Senior:** CPA with eight years in corporate accounting and audit. I run month-end close for a multi-entity group, keep reconciliations clean and like fixing the manual steps that slow a close down.

> **Manager or controller:** Accounting manager with twelve years of experience, leading a team of six through monthly close, annual audit and two ERP migrations. I set up close calendars and controls that hold up when the business grows.

### Credentials

Give credentials their own section, or list them under certifications, with the full name, the issuing board and the year. "Certified Public Accountant (CPA), Illinois Board of Examiners, 2019." If you hold more than one, such as a CPA and a CMA, list both. If you are still working toward one, say how far you are: "CPA candidate, passed FAR and AUD, BEC scheduled for May."

### Experience

List jobs newest first, with company, title, city and dates by month and year. Under each job, a short line of context helps when the company isn't well known: "Food manufacturer, five US entities, $350M revenue." Then three to five bullets for recent jobs and one to three for older ones.

### Skills

Split skills into accounting knowledge, systems and Excel. The side column of this layout keeps them short and visible. Put the items closest to the job post first.

### Education

Degree, university and year. A master's in accounting or taxation is worth listing, as is the number of credit hours if your state's license requires a certain number and you are still building toward it. Once you have a few years of experience, education needs no extra detail.

## Writing experience bullets

Good accounting bullets describe ownership and improvement. Did you own a part of the close? Did you shorten it, automate a step or clean up a messy account? Did the auditors find nothing in your areas?

A simple pattern that works: **what you own or did + the scale + what changed.**

- Weak: "Responsible for reconciliations."
- Better: "Reconcile 40 balance sheet accounts each month and cleared a two-year backlog of unreconciled items."

Use numbers when they are real: days in the close, number of entities, accounts or clients, audit adjustments, invoices processed. Don't round them up. Accountants are expected to be precise, and interviewers notice.

### Examples by level

**Staff accountant**

- Prepare monthly bank reconciliations for twelve accounts and clear reconciling items within the close
- Post accruals and prepaid amortization entries with supporting schedules
- Process about 600 vendor invoices a month and match them to purchase orders
- Built a simple tracker for missing receipts that cut open items at month end

**Senior accountant**

- Own month-end close for five entities and the intercompany eliminations
- Shortened the close from nine working days to six by moving accrual schedules into NetSuite
- Prepare audit schedules; the last two audits closed with no adjustments in my areas
- Review the work of two staff accountants and train new hires on the close checklist

**Manager or controller**

- Lead a team of six through monthly close, quarterly reporting and the annual audit
- Designed the close calendar and review controls used across three business units
- Managed the move from QuickBooks to NetSuite, including the chart of accounts redesign
- Present monthly results and variance explanations to the CFO and department heads

### Examples by specialty

**Audit**

- Ran fieldwork on audits for manufacturing and retail clients with revenue up to $400M
- Tested revenue, inventory and payroll controls and wrote the management letter points
- Supervised teams of two to four associates on each engagement

**Tax**

- Prepared federal and state corporate returns for 25 clients each year
- Researched sales tax obligations after a client started selling in new states
- Prepared quarterly estimated payments and tax provisions for the year-end audit

**Accounts payable and receivable**

- Ran weekly payment runs for about 300 vendors and kept duplicate payments at zero
- Reduced days sales outstanding by following up on invoices older than 60 days every week
- Set up vendor onboarding checks that stopped payments to unverified bank accounts

**Financial reporting**

- Prepare monthly financial statements and the board reporting package
- Drafted the revenue recognition memo when the company moved to subscription pricing
- Tie out every number in the quarterly report to the general ledger before release

**FP&A and cost accounting**

- Built the annual budget model for eight departments and run the monthly forecast
- Calculated standard costs for 150 products and explained variances to plant managers
- Created a margin report by customer that changed pricing on three large accounts

## Skills and keywords

Recruiters and tracking systems search for exact terms, so use the names that appear in job posts. Only list what you have used in real work.

| Category | Common examples |
| --- | --- |
| Standards | US GAAP, IFRS, ASC 606, ASC 842, SOX controls |
| Close and reporting | Month-end close, reconciliations, accruals, journal entries, financial statements, consolidation |
| Specialties | Accounts payable, accounts receivable, payroll, fixed assets, tax, audit, cost accounting |
| Systems | SAP, Oracle, NetSuite, Microsoft Dynamics, QuickBooks, Xero, BlackLine |
| Excel and data | Pivot tables, XLOOKUP, Power Query, Power BI |
| Credentials | CPA, CA, ACCA, CMA, CIA, EA |

For Excel, name the features you actually use instead of writing "advanced Excel." For soft skills, show them in your bullets: training staff, presenting results to managers or working with auditors.

## Tailoring your resume to a job post

A quick edit for each application usually beats sending the same file everywhere. It takes about ten minutes:

1. Read the job post and mark the standards, systems and tasks it mentions more than once.
2. Check that the ones you really have appear in your skills and in at least one bullet.
3. Move the bullets closest to the role to the top of each job. A tax role should see your tax work first.
4. Adjust your title line and the first sentence of the summary to match the role, for example "Staff Accountant" or "Senior Tax Accountant."
5. Remove older bullets that have nothing to do with the job if you need the space.

Use the job post's wording when it is true for you. If it says "account reconciliations" and you do them every month, say "account reconciliations."

## Formatting and length

One page is usual for staff and senior accountants. Use one font at 10 to 12 points, the same date format everywhere and right-aligned dates. Keep numbers in a consistent style: either "$1.2M" or "$1,200,000", not both.

Keep bullets to one or two lines each. If a bullet needs three lines, it usually holds two separate points, and splitting it makes both easier to read. Leave some white space between jobs so the dates and titles stand out when someone scans the page.

Send a PDF unless the employer asks for Word, and name the file clearly, for example "Priya-Raman-Senior-Accountant.pdf". If an employer uses a strict tracking system, the [ATS Friendly layout](/templates/software-engineer) puts everything in a single column.

## Why the sections are in this order

Experience comes first because what you own in the close matters more to an employer than where you studied. Education follows, with volunteering after it, since programs like free tax preparation show accounting work outside your job.

Skills, certifications and languages sit in the side column. That keeps your CPA and your systems visible next to every job you list, which is where a reader checks for them. If you are a recent graduate, move education to the top and look at the [student template](/templates/student) for how to present internships and coursework.

## Mistakes to avoid

- "Responsible for accounts payable" without any sense of volume or result
- Writing "CPA" when you are still a candidate
- A long list of software with no detail on what you did in it
- Numbers that don't add up or don't match each other. Check every figure on the page
- Confidential client names, if your engagement letter or employer policy doesn't allow it
- Dates in different formats across jobs
- Leaving out the reporting standard you work under, when a job post asks for US GAAP or IFRS
- Spelling mistakes in system names, such as "Netsuite" or "Quickbooks"

## Checklist before you send it

- Your credential appears in the title line and in the certifications section
- Every recent job says what you own and at what scale
- The standards and systems from the job post that you know appear on the page
- Every number is correct and written in the same style
- Dates and job titles are consistent
- The file is a PDF with your name in the file name
